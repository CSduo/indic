# Member announcement — what to send, and how

This file has two parts. The first is instructions for whoever sends this. The
second is the message itself, ready to use.

---

## Part 1 — Instructions for the sender

**What this is.** A one-off note to people who already have an account on
anvikshikijournal.in or who subscribed to the newsletter, telling them what the
site can now do. They opted in; this is not cold outreach and must not be
turned into any.

**Before sending, please:**

1. **Do not send to test or system accounts.** Anything that looks like a
   verification, automation or example address is not a person. Addresses at
   `example.com`, or containing `verify`, `test`, `codex` or a long numeric
   run, should be skipped.
2. **Send individually, not as one message with everyone in the To or Cc
   field.** Putting the whole list in one header exposes every member's address
   to every other member. Use one message per recipient, or a proper
   bulk-sending tool with individual delivery.
3. **Use the person's name where the message says `[NAME]`.** If there is no
   name on the account, use the greeting variant given below rather than
   writing "Hi there" or leaving a blank.
4. **Include an unsubscribe line.** It is at the bottom of the message and
   should not be removed.
5. **Send once.** If you are unsure whether it has already gone out, ask before
   sending rather than risking a second copy.

**Subject line** — pick one:

- `Ānvīkṣikī: notifications, messages, and following`
- `A few new things on Ānvīkṣikī`
- `Your Ānvīkṣikī account can do more now`

**Tone.** Plain and unhurried. This is a journal for careful reading, not a
product launch. No exclamation marks, no urgency, no "don't miss out".

---

## Part 2 — The message

**Greeting, with a name:**
> Dear [NAME],

**Greeting, when no name is on the account:**
> Dear reader,

---

A few things have been added to Ānvīkṣikī that you may find useful, and one of
them needs a single click from you before it works.

**Notifications.** You can now be told when something happens that concerns
you — a reply to a comment you left, an update on work you submitted, or a new
piece from a writer you follow. Nothing is sent unless you switch it on:

1. Sign in at https://anvikshikijournal.in
2. Open your account, then Notifications
3. Allow notifications when your browser asks

Your browser will ask once. If you dismiss it, it will not ask again, and the
setting has to be changed by hand afterwards — so it is worth deciding in the
moment.

**Following writers.** Every member now has a public profile listing their
published work. Follow someone, and when they publish, you hear about it. You
can find people through the Assembly, in the Community section, or from the
byline of anything you read.

**Messages.** Members can now write to each other directly, one to one or in a
group. Photographs and documents can be sent, and what is sent in a
conversation stays there — attachments are stored privately and are not
reachable by anyone outside it. If someone you have not met writes to you, it
arrives as a request rather than landing in your inbox, and nothing further
reaches you unless you accept.

**Reading.** A bar across the top of each piece shows how far through it you
are. Writers can see how many people read their work and how far they got —
never who. Reading here is not attributed to anyone.

None of this is required. The journal reads exactly as it did before if you
would rather leave it alone.

With regards,
Ānvīkṣikī

---
You are receiving this because you have an account at anvikshikijournal.in or
subscribed to our list. To stop receiving these, reply with "unsubscribe" and
you will be removed.

---

## Notes on what is deliberately absent

- **No recipient list is included in this file.** Addresses live in the admin
  panel and in the newsletter export; they do not belong in a document that
  gets passed between tools.
- **No claim that notifications are already on.** They are not. The permission
  has to be granted per browser, and saying otherwise would guarantee
  confusion when nothing arrives.
- **No suggestion that writers can see who read their work.** They cannot, and
  telling people they can — even loosely — would change what they feel able to
  read.
